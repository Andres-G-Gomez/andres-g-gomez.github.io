## How LLMs Could Help Nations Make Smarter Investments: A World Bank Use Case

Imagine you're a government official in a low- or middle-income country. Your budget is limited, but your responsibilities are vast—millions of citizens rely on you to make the right calls on healthcare, education, infrastructure, and employment. How do you decide where to allocate scarce resources to create the greatest possible impact?

For decades, the World Bank has helped countries make smarter development decisions through data. Today, some of that same data can be used to fine-tune large language models (LLMs), enabling these AI systems to help governments tackle exactly this kind of challenge—with unprecedented scale, speed, and contextual insight.

The Challenge: Smarter, Context-Aware Investment Planning

Traditional development planning is complex. It requires synthesizing:
* Local economic and demographic data
* Historical investment outcomes
* Community needs and infrastructure gaps
* Cultural and political realities

The question is no longer just what worked globally, but:
*"What will work best here, given our context and our people?"*

LLMs—especially when adapted to national contexts—offer a powerful tool to answer that.


### <ins> 3 Ways LLMs Could Guide Smarter Investments </ins>

Here are three approaches the World Bank and its partner nations could explore:

<ins> **Fine-Tuned National LLMs for Investment Q&A** </ins> 

   Idea:

    Fine-tune an LLM on country-specific financial reports, development plans, budgets, and World Bank data to create a smart assistant that can answer localized questions like:
    * *“What past investments improved access to education in rural regions?”*
    * *“Which areas are most underserved by public health facilities?”*

    Pros:
    * Deep contextual understanding of national priorities
    * Custom responses aligned with local languages, data, and policy frameworks


    Cons:
    * Requires substantial high-quality training data
    * Risk of outdated or biased outputs without ongoing curation

<br/>

<ins> **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** </ins> 

    Idea:

    Use an LLM that retrieves and reads national reports and real-time data from a searchable database, then generates grounded, up-to-date responses using those documents.


    Pros:
    * Avoids hallucinations by grounding responses in current, factual data
    * No expensive fine-tuning required
    * Easier to update across multiple countries


    Cons:
    * Dependent on well-organized and maintained data sources
    * May require complex engineering for robust and relevant retrieval

<br/>

<ins> **Scenario Simulation and Investment Ranking** </ins> 

    Idea:

    Treat the LLM as a policy simulation assistant. Feed it structured national data (e.g., demographics, infrastructure scores, historical ROI) and ask it to simulate the potential impact of different investment strategies:
    * *“What if we invested $10M in clean energy in coastal districts?”*
    * *“Which interventions would reduce poverty the most in the next 5 years?”*


    Pros:
    * Enables proactive scenario planning and forecasting
    * Helps governments prioritize interventions with the highest potential impact


    Cons:
    * LLMs are weak at causal reasoning without structured models
    * Requires strong grounding in empirical data and policy logic

<br/>

**Final Thoughts: Enabling Better Decisions with AI**

The promise of LLMs is not in replacing human judgment, but in amplifying human capacity—surfacing hidden insights, simulating trade-offs, and personalizing strategies to each nation’s context.
With careful attention to ethics, transparency, and local relevance, these AI tools could help governments allocate scarce resources more effectively—and improve the lives of millions.
