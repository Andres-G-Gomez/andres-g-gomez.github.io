## Building LLM Systems for Government Advisory: A Technical Starting Guide

Government agencies and NGOs increasingly need AI systems that can analyze reports from past infrastructure projects, learn from documented investment outcomes, and provide evidence-based recommendations for future initiatives. This post serves as a starting point for technical teams trying to build effective LLM systems for government advisory work, drawing from current research insights.

## Architecture Decision: RAG vs Fine-Tuning

**TL;DR: Start with RAG + instruction-tuned models, not fine-tuning.**

### Fine-Tuning

**What it is:** Further trains a pre-trained model on your own data to specialize it for a specific task, domain, or response style. Updates the model's internal weights to better match your use case.

**How it works:** Pre-trained model → Train on your dataset → Specialized model

**Pros:** High customization, no external dependencies, strong task-specific performance, learns new formats/styles

**Cons:** Costly, needs ML expertise, risk of forgetting general knowledge, harder to update

### RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

RAG enhances a language model's response by retrieving relevant information from external sources (like documents, databases, or knowledge bases) and including that information in the prompt. The model then generates responses based on both its training knowledge and the retrieved context.

**How it works:** Query → Retrieve relevant documents → Combine with prompt → Generate response

**Pros:** No retraining needed, easy to update knowledge, cost-effective, maintains model's general capabilities

**Cons:** Requires good retrieval system, limited by context window, potential for irrelevant retrievals

### When to use each:

- **RAG:** When you need up-to-date information, have changing knowledge bases, or want to maintain the model's general capabilities
- **Fine-tuning:** When you need specific response formats, domain expertise, or behavioral patterns (e.g., tone/style, response format) not achievable through prompting alone

While production systems often use both fine-tuning for response style and RAG for dynamic knowledge, beginning with RAG and an instruction-tuned model is a smart way to validate your setup before investing in fine-tuning.

## Common Challenges with Fine-Tuning Models for RAG Systems

Fine-tuning language models and integrating them into a RAG system poses certain challenges:

- **Retrieved context ignored:** Fine-tuned models may ignore the retrieved information, reducing the effectiveness of RAG
- **Catastrophic forgetting:** Models can lose instruction-following or reasoning abilities
- **Overfitting:** Models may fail to adapt to new or varied queries
- **Higher costs:** Fine-tuning and model hosting adds extra compute and engineering compared to off-the-shelf models

However, if you find yourself needing greater response format control, better domain-specific adaptation (e.g., enhanced technical terminology understanding), retrieval-specific behaviors (e.g., stronger reliance on retrieved context), or desire optimized performance, fine-tuning a model then integrating it into your RAG system may be your desired approach. If that is the case, there are several things to consider:

- **Design fine-tuning with retrieval in mind** — train on examples that include retrieved context, so the model learns to rely on external information
- **Use retrieval-aware techniques** — contrastive learning or prompt formatting that clearly separates retrieved content from the query
- **Start from an instruction-tuned base model** — to reduce the risk of catastrophic forgetting and preserve general capabilities
- **Consider lightweight fine-tuning methods like LoRA** — which allow task adaptation without fully overwriting the model's original behavior
- **Evaluate regularly and test prompts** — to catch issues early and ensure the model is using retrieval effectively
- **Start with RAG before fine-tuning** — build a solid baseline system, then layer in fine-tuning only if needed for specific behaviors or formats

### Technology Stack Recommendations

#### Vector Database Options:

- **Pinecone** – Fully managed, great for production, scalable and reliable
- **Weaviate** – Open-source, supports hybrid (vector + keyword) search, flexible data structure (easy to add new fields or object types)
- **Chroma** – Lightweight and fast, great for local dev and rapid prototyping

#### Embedding Models:

**Open Source:**
- **BGE-M3** - Excellent multilingual support, good for diverse document types
- **E5-mistral-7b-instruct** - Larger embedding model with strong performance, especially good if you're already using Mistral models
- **all-MiniLM-L6-v2** - Lightweight and fast, good baseline option
- **BGE-large-en-v1.5** - Strong performance for English documents
- **Nomic Embed v1.5** - Good balance of performance and efficiency

**Proprietary (via API):**
- **OpenAI text-embedding-3-large** - Currently one of the top performing
- **Cohere embed-english-v3.0** - Strong performance with good multilingual support
- **Voyage AI embeddings** - Specifically optimized for RAG applications

#### LLM Options:

The choice of LLM depends on your specific requirements around latency, cost, accuracy, and whether you need to host it on-site.

**Open-Source Models:**
- **Llama 3.1 (8B, 70B, 405B)** – Strong reasoning and instruction following, 8B is well suited for many RAG applications and resource efficient
- **Qwen2.5 (7B, 14B, 32B, 72B)** – Strong instruction following, good for structured data and code
- **Mixtral 8x7B Instruct** – Mixture-of-experts (MOE) model, efficient and high-performing for its size

**Proprietary Models (via API):**
- **GPT-4o** – Handles complex instructions, strong with retrieved context
- **Claude 3.5 Sonnet** – Strong reasoning, great instruction following and multi-source integration
- **Gemini 1.5 Pro** – Supports long contexts (up to 1M tokens), useful for large-scale RAG

## Step 2: Document Processing and Ingestion Pipeline

### Document Preprocessing Strategy

Government documents often come in varied formats and quality levels. Your preprocessing pipeline should handle:

#### Document Parsing:

- **PDF extraction** - Use tools like PyMuPDF or Adobe PDF Services API for complex layouts with tables and charts
- **OCR for scanned documents** - Tesseract or cloud services (Azure Document Intelligence, AWS Textract) for legacy reports
- **Structured data handling** - Parse tables, appendices, and metadata separately to preserve context

#### Text Chunking Best Practices:

Government reports benefit from semantic chunking rather than simple character limits:

- **Section-aware chunking** - Respect document structure (executive summaries, findings, recommendations)
- **Overlap strategy** - 100-200 character overlap between chunks to maintain context
- **Metadata preservation** - Include document type, date, agency, project ID with each chunk

#### Quality Control Pipeline:

- **Content filtering** - Remove headers/footers, page numbers, and boilerplate text
- **Duplicate detection** - Government documents often contain repeated sections across reports
- **Language detection** - Handle multilingual documents if working with international agencies

## Step 3: Retrieval Strategy for Government Use Cases

### Hybrid Search Approach

Pure vector search often misses important government-specific information. Implement hybrid retrieval:

#### Vector + Keyword Search:

- **Vector search** for conceptual similarity ("infrastructure resilience" matching "climate adaptation")
- **Keyword search** for exact matches (project codes, legislation references, specific metrics)
- **Weighted combination** - Typically 70% vector, 30% keyword for government documents

#### Metadata Filtering:

Government queries often need contextual constraints:

```python
# Example: Find similar projects from the same time period and budget range
filters = {
    "date_range": "2018-2022",
    "budget_min": 10000000,
    "agency": "Department of Transportation",
    "project_status": "completed"
}
```

#### Multi-Stage Retrieval:

1. **Initial broad retrieval** - Cast wide net for relevant documents
2. **Re-ranking** - Use cross-encoder models to improve relevance
3. **Diversity filtering** - Ensure results span different projects/agencies to avoid bias

## Step 4: Prompt Engineering for Government Advisory

### Context Management

Government LLM systems need structured prompts that handle:

#### Source Attribution:

```
Based on the following government reports and data:

[DOCUMENT 1: Infrastructure Assessment 2023, Department of Transportation]
{retrieved_content_1}

[DOCUMENT 2: Highway Resilience Study, Federal Highway Administration] 
{retrieved_content_2}

Question: {user_query}

Provide recommendations with specific citations to source documents. If information conflicts between sources, acknowledge the discrepancy and explain which source appears more authoritative.
```

#### Evidence-Based Reasoning:

- Structure prompts to require citing specific data points
- Ask for confidence levels in recommendations
- Request identification of information gaps or uncertainties

## Success Metrics

Track these KPIs to measure system effectiveness:

- **Response Quality:** Expert ratings on 1-5 scale
- **Source Attribution:** % of claims with proper citations
- **User Adoption:** Weekly active users, session length
- **Confidence Calibration:** How well confidence scores predict accuracy
- **Policy Impact:** Track which recommendations get implemented

## Getting Started Today

1. **Quick prototype:** Use OpenAI API + Pinecone + 50 policy documents
2. **Test core hypothesis:** Can your system answer 10 realistic policy questions?
3. **Measure baseline:** How do responses compare to human expert analysis?
4. **Iterate rapidly:** Improve prompts based on expert feedback
5. **Scale gradually:** Add documents and features based on user needs

Remember: The goal isn't to replace policy experts, but to empower them. Build systems that amplify human judgment rather than replacing it.
